# Slow tasting, without pretension

## A three-sip experience

1. Before drinking: fragrance/aroma — does it suggest citrus peel, stone fruit, cocoa, florals, toast? "No clear note" is acceptable.
2. First sip: perceived sweetness, acidity and bitterness *in balance*, weight or silkiness on tongue. Describe texture separately from concentration.
3. After swallowing: finish length, nature of finish (clean, drying, chocolate, fruit) and possible returning sweetness 回甘. As it cools or ice melts, ask what changes.

For milk drinks record milk choice and sweetness/added syrup separately. For espresso drink record espresso extraction style (ristretto/normal/lungo) when known; for filters record method (V60, espresso, AeroPress, cold brew) when known.

## Learning through contrast

- Astringency is drying/puckering, not the same as bitterness.
- Acidity can be juicy and pleasant, not necessarily sour or defective.
- Sweetness in plain black coffee is subtle perceived sweetness; it need not mean sugar content.
- Aftertaste length ≠ aftertaste quality. "Long, harsh finish" and "long, sweet finish" are distinct.
- Natural fermentation flavor descriptors don't imply flavorings.
- Long espresso extraction may increase extracted compounds but brand-specific parameters and brew strength matter; never guarantee which has more caffeine without recipe measurements.

## Coffee and energy caution

Do not frame caffeine as a source of indefinitely sustained energy or scientifically proven introspection; effects vary. If insomnia, pregnancy, anxiety or sensitivity is mentioned, consider decaf/half-caf, smaller servings, earlier timing and user guidance without diagnosing or making medical claims. Caffeine quantity is unknown without recipes; added shots may increase dose. No pressure to consume caffeine.
