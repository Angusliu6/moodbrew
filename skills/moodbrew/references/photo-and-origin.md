# Photo and origin interpreting workflow

## Visible evidence first

1. Name the kind of image (menu / coffee bag / barista card / brewed cup / screenshot). Extract visible option names, price, country, region, microregion, farm/estate/producer, variety, processing, roast, tasting notes, brew choice, altitude, date *only where readable*. Quoted text should preserve label spelling. If blurry, state missing detail.
2. If the picture has multiple choices, compare only those photographed. Note things unknown such as serving method or whether an option is sold out. Ask about one decisive missing constraint only when needed.
3. Never diagnose roast level, beans' country or flavor by drink's visible color, crema or cup design. Photo recognition of a label is not a sensory tasting; treat written flavor notes as vendor descriptions.
4. When barista gives facts in user text, label "店员介绍/你提供"; can be useful without claiming independent verification.
5. Present a short origin story grounded in the known place. If access to web is available and user wants precise farm facts, check authoritative roaster, farm or regional sources and cite. Avoid invented histories; don't confuse an importer/roaster name with the growing estate.

## Four layers for coffee stories

- **Geography**: where this coffee grew *if printed*. Country ≠ farm; a city may be roasting location.
- **Production**: variety, altitude, shade, soil, rainfall, harvest conditions (only if known/verified). Climate can shape production but cannot reliably predict a given cup alone.
- **Processing**: washed often cleaner/clearer; natural often fruit-forward/heavier; honey can offer sweetness/body; fermentations can vary widely. These are tendencies with overlap, not guarantees.
- **Cup**: roaster's claimed notes vs user's tasted sensations. Orange, jasmine, chocolate descriptors usually mean sensory associations, not added ingredients. Processing, roast, brew and storage substantially influence the experience.

## Region cheat sheet — probabilistic, not farm proof

- Ethiopia: many specialty lots with floral, tea-like, citrus or berry notes; enormous diversity. Yirgacheffe vs Sidama vs Guji not interchangeable.
- Kenya: some washed high-altitude lots show vivid berry/currant-like acidity and layered sweetness; many counterexamples.
- Colombia: huge range; washed lots often balanced fruit, caramel or citrus; region and process matter more than national stereotype.
- Brazil: many lots with chocolate, nutty or rounded body; numerous fruit-forward experimental lots too.
- Panama: Geisha/Gesha can show florals/tea/citrus under some conditions; not all Panama coffee is Geisha nor every Geisha expensive.
- Indonesia: regional/wet-hulled coffees may have earthy/herbal/heavier character; washed/natural lots differ markedly.
- Costa Rica: honey processes and clean washed lots often balanced sweetness and fruit; many variations.
- Rwanda / Burundi: various washed coffees can be citrus/stone-fruit/tea-like, but not universal.

## How to narrate an origin naturally

"标签写的是【埃塞俄比亚·Guji，日晒】。Guji 位于埃塞俄比亚南部；如果这批豆保持典型的日晒果香，你可能会发现莓果或熟果般的甜香。但真正的杯测结果还要看品种、处理批次、烘焙和冲煮。菜单上的风味词是【原文】，我没看到庄园名称，所以不能断言它来自某个特定庄园。"

## Compare in context

For choice support, rank only available photographed beans by fit to today (mood, flavor preferences, coffee method, budget, novelty). Output: **我会选 A，因为 ... / 如果想大胆尝试 B，因为 ... / 不确定的地方 ...**. Prefer trying 1/2 cup or changing brew before buying a full bag if possible.
